# Languages (D169, D193)

TRMNL Games speaks English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Polish and Dutch. English is the source; every other language is a full translation written in the repository. Translation never changes game state: the simulator, stored logs, item rows and catalogs stay English, and every language is applied as something is shown.

The first three arrived with D169. D193 added the other five, chosen from the languages TRMNL's own app speaks rather than from current players' settings, since a new language is how new players find the game. They share the Latin script and the fonts already on the device; Cyrillic, Hebrew and the CJK languages need fonts and, for Hebrew, a right-to-left layout first.

## Rollout

`LANGUAGES_LIVE` in `packages/platform/src/locale.ts` is the switch. While it is false the picker is hidden, browsers' languages are ignored and TRMNL screens stay English; only an explicit `tg_locale` cookie changes the companion, which is how pages are tested before launch. It went on on 2026-10-15; set it back to false to return everyone to English.

## Which language

- **Companion and site.** The `tg_locale` cookie, else the browser's `Accept-Language` (`localeFromAcceptLanguage`). The root route resolves it on the server, renders `<html lang>` with it, and the browser reads it back on client navigation (`documentLocale`). The footer's picker writes the cookie and, signed in, `users.language`, then reloads.
- **The browser does not translate the page (D173).** Chrome's translator wraps text in `<font>` and React then crashes on the next update. The document sets `translate="no"`, the `notranslate` class and `<meta name="google" content="notranslate">`. The picker is how the language changes.
- **A signed-in player's choice follows them.** `LanguageSync` compares `users.language` with the page's language and, when they differ, writes the cookie and reloads once per session.
- **Device and alerts.** `deviceLocale(user)`: English while `LANGUAGES_LIVE` is false, then `ownerLocale(user)`: `users.language`, else the TRMNL account's language (`users.trmnlLocale`, D168; `pt-BR` and `es-ES` both match), else English.

## Companion copy

Each page keeps its copy in a catalog in `apps/web/src/i18n/`, made with `defineMessages({ en, fr, de, es, it, pt, pl, nl })`. Every other language is typed against English, so a missing or misshapen translation fails the typecheck. Copy that needs numbers or names is a function (`open: (game) => ...`). A component reads its catalog with `useT(catalog)`; a route head uses `messagesFor(catalog, match.context.locale)`. Clerk's own screens use `@clerk/localizations` (`CLERK_LOCALIZATIONS` in the root route).

- **Polish counts** take three noun forms (1 przedmiot, 2–4 przedmioty, 5+ przedmiotów). `plForm(n, one, few, many)` picks one; a table of `[one, other]` forms takes a third entry for Polish and is read with `countForm(forms, n)`.
- **The tab bar** shares 390 px between five labels in a pixel font, so each stays about six characters ("Wpisy", "Tabela", "Podio").
- **Fonts.** The pixel fonts ship their Latin and Latin Extended subsets by `unicode-range`, so Polish letters (ą ę ł ś ż) stay in the pixel face.
- **Dates and numbers** format through `INTL_TAG` (`es-ES`, `it-IT`, `pt-BR`, `pl-PL`, `nl-NL`), never a per-page map.
- **TRMNL's own buttons** are named the way TRMNL's app names them in that language (usetrmnl/trmnl-i18n `web_ui`): Instalar / Installa / Instalar / „Instaluj” / Installeren, Guardar / Salva / Salvar / „Zapisz” / Opslaan, Configurar / Configura / Configurar / „Konfiguruj” / Configureer.
- The changelog, FAQ, help guide, privacy policy and terms are written out per language; the legal pages say the English text prevails.

## Game content

`packages/desk-crawler/src/i18n/` translates catalog text, names and stored log summaries, keyed by the English text itself, so one dictionary covers every catalog version and every log already written. Each language has a dictionary file with the same keys as French.

- `nouns`: names that take articles (monsters, areas, gear, bags, potions, materials), with their gender (`m`, `f`, `n`, `pl`, `fpl`). To-do labels' plurals ("Paper Imps") are entries of their own.
- `adjectives`: rarity and affix words. Item names are parsed into affix, rarity, gear and workbench suffix ("Vampiric Rare Keyboard Mace Mk II +3") and rebuilt. French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese put the adjectives after the noun and agree them (`[m, f]`); German, Dutch and Polish put them first: German with case endings, Dutch with its bare or inflected form (`[base, inflected]`, bare after *een* with a neuter noun), Polish with `[m, f, n, pl]`.
- `text`: everything else. An entry with `{vars}` is a template. A stored line that matches its English side is refilled into the localized side, where a name asks for an article form (`grammar.ts`, `ARTICLE_FORMS`):
  - French `{un:…}`, `{le:…}`, `{au:…}`; German `{einen:…}`, `{im:…}`, `{nom:…}`;
  - Spanish `{el:…}`, `{un:…}`, `{del:…}`, `{al:…}`, `{en:…}`; Italian `{il:…}`, `{nel:…}`, `{del:…}`, `{al:…}`, `{dal:…}` with elision (l’Archivio, un’Auto, lo Scarabeo); Portuguese `{o:…}`, `{um:…}`, `{do:…}`, `{no:…}`, `{ao:…}`, `{pelo:…}`; Dutch `{de:…}` (de/het), `{een:…}`, `{geen:…}`; Polish names stay in the nominative (`{nom:…}`) after a colon or in brackets.
  - A capital (`{Un:monster}`) starts a sentence, and `{ein:monster|Elite-}` / `{Een:monster|Elite-}` adds a compound prefix.
  - Agreement endings follow the hero or a name: `{e:hero}` in French, `{o:monster}` in Spanish and Italian, `{oa:hero}` in Portuguese (where `o` is the article), `{a:hero}` and `{y:monster}` in Polish. `agreesWithHero(locale)` says whether the companion needs the hero's gender.
- Localized templates leave out the numbers the chips show (`+{xp} XP`), and a consequence that is only numbers ("Lost 12 gold.") translates to nothing.
- A line that matches nothing stays English rather than coming out half translated. `tests/sim/i18n.test.ts` simulates five catalog versions and requires every story to translate in every language with every marked name kept.

`logPresentation(entry, { locale, heroGender })` returns the translated narrative and chips in the reader's language; `chipLocale: 'en'` keeps English chip units where the device template keys its marks on them.

### Style

- French uses *vous*; German, Spanish, Italian, Polish and Dutch the informal *du*, *tú*, *tu*, *ty*, *je*; Brazilian Portuguese *você*.
- Log lines have no subject, as in English: French and Portuguese past tense ("A vaincu…", "Derrotou…"), Spanish preterite ("Derrotó a…"), Italian *passato prossimo* agreeing with the hero ("Ha sconfitto…"), German and Dutch participle-last ("Am Drucker eine [[Tacker-Mimik]] besiegt.", "Bij de printer een [[Nietmachinemimic]] verslagen."), Polish with the name as subject or after a colon ("[[Zszywaczowy mimik]] pokonany przy drukarce.", "Przybycie: [[Archiwum]].").
- Avoid participles that would agree with an unknown player (raid rivals are names, not genders); where a participle follows the hero it carries the agreement token.
- German and Dutch names are compounds; Polish names stay in the nominative, so lines are built around them.

## The TRMNL screen

`buildPayload({ …, locale })` tells everything the screen says in the owner's language: stories and to-do labels through the dictionaries, item, area, stance and monster names, and the status, attention, notice, celebration, QR and recap lines from `packages/desk-crawler/src/i18n/device.ts`. Area names keep their bold marks with the article outside them (`deviceArea` with `AREA_FORM`: "la [[Salle des serveurs]]", "im [[Serverraum]]", "nella [[Sala server]]", "Serwerownia"). A change the template draws as a mark (XP, gold, HP, potions, bag slots) keeps its English unit, since only its number shows; a change spelled out in words (an effect, "Pouch holds 4") reads in the owner's language.

The template's own words come from the payload's `labels` (template v57), each read with its English text as the Liquid default, so a template that meets an older payload still reads in English. Desk Crawler's two push alerts (`alertMessage` in `apps/backend/convex/lib/alerts.ts`) use the same locale.

Preview a language with `pnpm tsx tools/trmnl/preview.ts --portrait --recap --keepsakes --inline-art --locale es`, then `pnpm sweep:trmnl <out.json> --filter es.`, and again with `--no-title-bar`. The tight spots: the keepsake title (label, word and code together no longer than "Keepsake code 482 917", and narrow layouts show the label alone, hence "Recuerdo: 482 917"), the full landscape travelling line, which shares its row with the hearts ("Hacia …, llegada 10:30"), and the quarter layout's recap title, which shares a line with its hours ("La noche 19:00-07:00").

Idle Angler and Paper Lives, hidden in production, stay English on the device and in the companion for now.

## Adding content

A new English line needs its entry in every dictionary in the same change. `tests/sim/i18n.test.ts` checks that all dictionaries cover the same English lines as French, use only known article forms, translate every simulated story and keep every marked name.
