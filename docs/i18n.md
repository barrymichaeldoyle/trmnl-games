# Languages (D169)

TRMNL Games speaks English, French and German. English is the source; French and German are full translations written in the repository. Translation never changes game state: the simulator, stored logs, item rows and catalogs stay English, and every language is applied as something is shown.

## Rollout

`LANGUAGES_LIVE` in `packages/platform/src/locale.ts` is the switch. While it is false the picker is hidden, browsers' languages are ignored and TRMNL screens stay English; only an explicit `tg_locale` cookie (`fr` or `de`) changes the companion, which is how pages are tested before launch. Flip it once the companion, the public pages and the device are translated and the preview sweep passes in every language.

## Which language

- **Companion and site.** The `tg_locale` cookie, else the browser's `Accept-Language` (`localeFromAcceptLanguage`). The root route resolves it on the server, renders `<html lang>` with it, and the browser reads it back on client navigation (`documentLocale`). The footer's picker writes the cookie and, signed in, `users.language`, then reloads.
- **A signed-in player's choice follows them.** `LanguageSync` compares `users.language` with the page's language and, when they differ, writes the cookie and reloads once per session.
- **Device and alerts.** `deviceLocale(user)`: English while `LANGUAGES_LIVE` is false, then `ownerLocale(user)`: `users.language`, else the TRMNL account's language (`users.trmnlLocale`, D168), else English.

## Companion copy

Each page keeps its copy in a catalog in `apps/web/src/i18n/`, made with `defineMessages({ en, fr, de })`. French and German are typed against English, so a missing or misshapen translation fails the typecheck. Copy that needs numbers or names is a function (`open: (game) => ...`). A component reads its catalog with `useT(catalog)`; a route head uses `messagesFor(catalog, match.context.locale)`. Clerk's own screens use `@clerk/localizations`, loaded only for a French or German reader.

## Game content

`packages/desk-crawler/src/i18n/` translates catalog text, names and stored log summaries, keyed by the English text itself, so one dictionary covers every catalog version and every log already written.

- `nouns`: names that take articles (monsters, areas, gear, bags, potions, materials), with their gender. To-do labels' plurals ("Paper Imps") are entries of their own.
- `adjectives`: rarity and affix words. Item names are parsed into affix, rarity, gear and workbench suffix ("Vampiric Rare Keyboard Mace Mk II +3") and rebuilt: French puts the adjectives after the noun and agrees them; German puts them first with the ending the case and article need.
- `text`: everything else. An entry with `{vars}` is a template. A stored line that matches its English side is refilled into the localized side, where a name asks for an article form: `{un:monster}`, `{le:destination}`, `{au:destination}` in French; `{einen:monster}`, `{im:destination}`, `{nom:item}` in German; a capital (`{Un:monster}`) starts a sentence and `{ein:monster|Elite-}` adds a compound prefix. `{e:monster}` and `{e:hero}` are French agreement endings, the hero's from `heroGender`.
- Localized templates leave out the numbers the chips show (`+{xp} XP`), and a consequence that is only numbers ("Lost 12 gold.") translates to nothing.
- A line that matches nothing stays English rather than coming out half translated. `tests/sim/i18n.test.ts` simulates five catalog versions and requires every story to translate in both languages.

`logPresentation(entry, { locale, heroGender })` returns the translated narrative and chips in the reader's language; `chipLocale: 'en'` keeps English chip units where the device template keys its marks on them.

### Style

- French uses *vous*, German *du*. Log lines have no subject, as in English: French "A vaincu une [[Mimique agrafeuse]]…", German participle-last "Am Drucker eine [[Tacker-Mimik]] besiegt."
- French avoids participles that would agree with an unknown player (raid rivals are names, not genders); where a participle follows the hero it carries `{e:hero}`.
- German names are compounds, so they decline without adjective endings; items stand in nominative positions ("Gefunden: [[Seltener Brieföffner]]").
- Glossary: hero *héros* / *Held*; HP *PV* / *LP*; gold *or* / *Gold*; level *niveau* / *Stufe*; bag *sac* / *Tasche*; stance *posture* / *Haltung*; leaderboard *classement* / *Rangliste*; keepsake *souvenir* / *Andenken*; workbench *établi* / *Werkbank*; desk raid *raid de bureau* / *Schreibtisch-Überfall*; companion *compagnon* / *Begleit-App*.

## The TRMNL screen

`buildPayload({ …, locale })` tells everything the screen says in the owner's language: stories and to-do labels through the dictionaries, item, area, stance and monster names, and the status, attention, notice, celebration, QR and recap lines from `packages/desk-crawler/src/i18n/device.ts`. Area names keep their bold marks with the article outside them (`deviceArea`: "la [[Salle des serveurs]]", "im [[Serverraum]]"). A change the template draws as a mark (XP, gold, HP, potions, bag slots) keeps its English unit, since only its number shows; a change spelled out in words (an effect, "Pouch holds 4") reads in the owner's language.

The template's own words come from the payload's `labels` (template v57), each read with its English text as the Liquid default, so a template that meets an older payload still reads in English. Desk Crawler's two push alerts (`alertMessage` in `apps/backend/convex/lib/alerts.ts`) use the same locale. Preview a language with `pnpm tsx tools/trmnl/preview.ts --portrait --recap --inline-art --locale fr`, then `pnpm sweep:trmnl <out.json> --filter fr.`.

Idle Angler and Paper Lives, hidden in production, stay English on the device and in the companion for now.

## Adding content

A new English line needs its French and German entries in the same change. `tests/sim/i18n.test.ts` checks that both dictionaries cover the same English lines, use only known article forms and translate every simulated story.
